# Friction

Friction is **a contact force that opposes the relative motion or attempted motion between two surfaces in contact**. [[1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UcQIEKfZaxM&t=1)]

## 1. Types of Friction

- **Static (Limiting) Friction** ($F_s$): The maximum opposing force that acts between two surfaces when they are at rest, just before motion begins.
- **Kinetic (Dynamic) Friction** ($F_k$): The opposing force that acts when one surface is actively sliding or moving over another at a uniform speed.
- *Note:* The coefficient of static friction is always greater than the coefficient of kinetic friction ($\mu_s > \mu_k$) because it takes more force to break an object free from rest than to keep it moving. [[1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-QLtOJn3drM&t=337), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nedAgp31Qj8&t=377)]

## 2. Key Formulas and Laws

- **Frictional Force:** $F = \mu R$
  - $F$ = Frictional force
  - $\mu$ = Coefficient of friction (no units)
  - $R$ (or $N$) = Normal reaction (on a flat horizontal plane, $R = mg$) [[1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bSaU778nbxQ&t=9), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UcQIEKfZaxM&t=1)]
- **Inclined Plane:**
  - Normal reaction: $R = mg \cos\theta$
  - Parallel weight component: $W = mg \sin\theta$
  - Relationship to angle of friction ($\theta$): $\mu = \tan\theta$ [[1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bSaU778nbxQ&t=9)]
- **Key Law:** Frictional force is directly proportional to the normal reaction and is independent of the surface area in contact, provided the normal reaction stays constant. [[1](https://www.scribd.com/document/655588281/jamb-physics-2000), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bSaU778nbxQ&t=9)]

## 3. Advantages, Disadvantages, and Reduction

- **Advantages:** Walking without slipping, writing with a pen, driving and stopping cars with brakes, holding objects firmly.
- **Disadvantages:** Wear and tear in machine parts, generation of unwanted heat, loss of energy efficiency.
- **Methods of Reduction:** Using lubricants (oil/grease), ball bearings, streamlining, and polishing rough surfaces. [[1](https://studentfirst.ng/jamb/physics/topics/friction), [2](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BDQB51cmA_g&t=10)]

## 4. Fluids, Viscosity, and Terminal Velocity

- **Viscosity:** The internal friction or resistance of a fluid (liquid or gas) to flow.
- **Terminal Velocity:** The constant maximum velocity attained by a body falling through a viscous fluid when the downward gravitational force equals the sum of the upward buoyant force and the viscous drag.
- **Stokes' Law:** Relates the viscous drag force on a small spherical body falling through a viscous liquid to its radius, velocity, and the viscosity of the fluid. [[1](https://miyagilabs.ai/ng/jamb/physics/notes/A6), [2](https://myschool.ng/classroom/physics?topic=friction)]
